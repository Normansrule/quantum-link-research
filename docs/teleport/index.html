Teleportation, one step at a time

Scroll. The panel on the left is the actual eight-amplitude state of three qubits, computed exactly (docs/js/teleport_core.js, checked against Qiskit to 10⁻¹⁶). Bar length is probability, colour is phase. Choose the state Alice sends and the outcome she measures.

step 0

Alice holds a state she does not know

q0 carries |ψ⟩ = cos(θ/2)|0⟩ + e^{iφ} sin(θ/2)|1⟩. Choose it; Alice never learns θ or φ, and no measurement could tell her.

step 1

A Bell pair is shared

H then CNOT makes (|00⟩ + |11⟩)/√2 between q1 (Alice) and q2 (Bob). In the real link this pair is made at a relay and its halves travel as photons; it carries no information about |ψ⟩.

step 2

Alice rotates into the Bell basis

CNOT from q0 to q1, then H on q0. Look at the bars: all eight amplitudes are now non-zero, and every one of Bob's four possible states already contains |ψ⟩, up to a Pauli.

step 3

Alice measures: two classical bits

Each outcome has probability exactly ¼ whatever |ψ⟩ is, which is why the bits reveal nothing about it. Pick the outcome:

step 4

The bits travel at the speed of light

Until they arrive, Bob's qubit, averaged over Alice's four equally likely outcomes, is the maximally mixed state: its Bloch vector is zero. That is the no-signalling theorem in one line, and it is why nothing can go faster than the classical channel.

step 5

Bob corrects and holds |ψ⟩

X if m1 = 1, Z if m0 = 1. Fidelity with Alice's original state: 1. The original on q0 was destroyed by the measurement, so nothing was cloned. With a noisy pair of fraction f, the average fidelity would be (2f+1)/3 (qll/circuits/teleportation.py).
