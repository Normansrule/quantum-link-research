Link budget · flagship F3 · REQ-CAP-001

A trillion pairs a second leave Earth. How many reach Mars?

Every stage of the link multiplies the rate by a factor from a tested model: optics, the atmosphere, the spreading of the beam over hundreds of millions of kilometres, pointing jitter, detectors, memories, and the Sun getting in the way. Sunlight reflected by Earth competes with the signal at the Mars receiver, and every herald it causes stores noise. Then the pair must wait in a memory for Earth's two classical bits. A memory is paid for twice here: its retrieval efficiency at both ends multiplies the rate, and its coherence time sets the fidelity after the wait. Assumptions (all adjustable in MarsLinkDesign): 1550 nm, transmit optics 0.8, receive optics 0.7, detectors 0.9, Earth elevation 40° and the Sun 12° below the horizon for a ground station, weather and operations duty 0.5, storage for one round trip, depolarizing memories, Earth's near-infrared albedo 0.3, airglow 10⁻⁷ W m⁻² sr⁻¹ nm⁻¹.
