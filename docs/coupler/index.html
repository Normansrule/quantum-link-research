Coupler lab · learn 02/22 · proposal E16

Switch an interaction off. Then use it for a gate.

Two superconducting qubits never fully stop talking: a residual ZZ makes one qubit's frequency depend on the other's state. A tunable coupler cancels it at one frequency, the idle point; a flux pulse turns it up for a controlled-Z (CZ) gate. Every curve here is computed by the tested Python models (qll/hardware/tunable_coupler.py, cz_gate.py, qll/circuits/zz_ramsey.py) and written to site_data.json.

why the shape matters

Shaped smoothly in the |11⟩–|20⟩ mixing angle (after Martinis and Geller), the pulse slows down exactly where the two levels are closest, and the population swinging into |20⟩ all comes back: — left behind. With the same 15 ns ramps shaped linearly in frequency and recalibrated to π, — is stranded in |20⟩: leakage, the error that fast adiabatic pulses are designed to avoid.

Hybridization is not leakage: at the interaction point the dressed |11⟩ is 17 % bare |20⟩, and the bar returns to zero when the pulse ends. What counts is what is left at the end. Idealized: coherent evolution only, no T₁ or T₂.
