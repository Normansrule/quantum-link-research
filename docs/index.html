Can Earth and Mars share a secret no computer can read?

Physics says yes, with one catch: entanglement carries no message by itself, and the two classical bits every teleportation needs take 3 to 22 minutes at the speed of light. This project works out, in code that is tested against the equations, what that catch costs, what hardware could pay it, and what a student can build on the way.

The idea in six sentences

  1. A qubit is an arrow on a sphere. Noise shortens it (T₁) and blurs its direction (T₂).
  2. Heat is noise you can compute. n̄ = 1/(e^{ħω/kT} − 1): microwave qubits need 15 mK; optical photons are already quiet at room temperature.
  3. Photons are the only thing that travels. Fiber loses them exponentially, free space only as 1/L². Past a few hundred km, the sky wins.
  4. Entanglement is a resource, not a radio. Bob's results look random until Alice's two bits arrive; the code refuses to read a teleported state early.
  5. The memory must outlive the round trip. 6–45 minutes to Mars and back. Diamond lasts a minute, ions an hour, rare-earth crystals 13 hours.
  6. Everything is checked against an equation. Every module cites a paper, every number traces to a requirement, every result has a test.

Three experiments, one physics

F1

Two computers, one city

Heralded entanglement and teleportation over 25 km of deployed fiber. Round trip 0.25 ms. Done in the field in 2024; our version starts on a $5k bench.

pass: herald > 1 Hz · S > 2.2 · F > 0.7 F2

Earth to satellite

A single-photon downlink from low Earth orbit; loss 10⁻⁶ per pass. Micius did it in 2017, a 23 kg microsatellite in 2025; our version reproduces their budgets, then a rooftop link.

pass: budget within ±3 dB · QBER < 5% at 10° sun angle F3

Earth to Mars

The same protocol with a 6–45 minute classical round trip and 10⁻⁹ loss. Done nowhere. Our version: delayed-bits bench, relay scheduling on real pass data, a messenger that fails closed.

pass: F > 2/3 after a stored 22-minute delay
