# Chapter 4 — Results (draft; every number from `results/tables.md`)

## 4.1 Temperature decides where components live

A 5 GHz superconducting qubit sees about 1250 thermal photons per mode at room temperature and about 10⁻⁷ at 15 mK, whereas a 193 THz telecom photon sees 4 × 10⁻¹⁴ at room temperature. The optical carrier is therefore the only carrier that can cross a warm channel, and any microwave processor at a network node must either be transduced to optical wavelengths or kept behind a local optical interconnect. This single computation motivates the design stance, adopted in Chapter 6, of building the link around platforms with native optical transitions.

## 4.2 Circuits (Phase 2)

Ideal teleportation reproduces the input to a fidelity of 1 within numerical precision; with a Werner resource of fully entangled fraction f the Haar-averaged fidelity follows (2f+1)/3 to within 2 × 10⁻³, crossing the classical limit of 2/3 at f = 1/2. The CHSH value of the ideal pair is 2√2 to 10⁻⁶, and the Werner formula 2√2(4f−1)/3 holds to 10⁻⁹. Entanglement swapping of two Werner pairs reproduces the Briegel recurrence F² + (1−F)²/3 to 10⁻⁹. The three canonical noise channels agree with QuTiP master-equation solutions to 10⁻⁵, and the finite-temperature channel reduces to zero-temperature damping when the bath occupation vanishes.

## 4.3 Links (Phase 3)

The exact Gaussian-over-aperture law recovers the 1/L² far-field scaling and shows that the uniform-disc estimate used in early drafts under-counts collected power by exactly a factor of two. With the reported 10 µrad divergence, a 1.2 m receiver, 1.2 µrad pointing, and representative atmospheric and optical factors, the model gives a two-downlink Micius loss of 66 dB at 30° elevation and 79 dB at 15°, inside the reported 64–82 dB. The BB84 threshold is 11.00 %; every repeaterless protocol rate implemented (decoy-state BB84, MDI) sits below the PLOB capacity at 10⁻¹, 10⁻³, and 10⁻⁶ transmittance, while the twin-field rate crosses it at a few hundred kilometres of fiber.

The two-site laboratory key link of flagship F1 (the SEE 510 case study, `systems/see510/`) is simulated end to end with BB84 and an ideal single-photon source, a fiber of configurable length, detectors with finite efficiency and dark counts, an intercept-and-resend adversary, Cascade reconciliation with hash verification, privacy amplification with a Hoeffding margin on the error rate, an HMAC-authenticated classical channel, and key delivery to an AES-256-GCM application. Twelve validation cases match closed-form detection and error-rate models within statistical error and reproduce exactly from their seeds. With 10⁶ pulses per session at 1 MHz, InGaAs-class detectors (efficiency 0.2, dark counts 10⁻⁶), and 1 % misalignment, the link yields about 45,000 key bits per second at 0 km and 11,600 at 25 km. The error rate stays near 1 % until dark counts compete with the signal near 230 km, yet a 10⁶-pulse block stops yielding key between 75 and 100 km, because too few detections remain for estimation and amplification; ten-times-longer blocks yield key at 125 km. Interception raises the error rate by a quarter of the intercepted fraction. The operator is alerted from about 10 %, the session is rejected above about 40 %, and accepted sessions lose more bits to privacy amplification than the adversary knew. Ordinary noise and an adversary tuned to the same error rate are indistinguishable by error rate and detection rate, which is why the protocol charges every error to an adversary. Two refinements remove the model's weakest assumptions. First, the classical channel is authenticated information-theoretically, with Wegman–Carter tags over each site's view of the whole transcript checked before any key is accepted. The 381 bits of pre-shared key each session spends are replaced from its output, so a 10⁶-pulse session near 75 km is accepted but nets no key. Second, with an attenuated laser in place of the ideal source, a photon-number-splitting adversary leaves the error rate and signal gain unchanged. Without decoys, the worst-case analysis guarantees no key at 25 km, and an analysis that ignored multi-photon pulses would keep about 74,000 bits of a block in which she knew about 74,000. With signal, decoy, and vacuum intensities, her attack drives the decoy gain about 30 standard deviations below its honest value, the operator is alerted, and the key shrinks from about 26,000 to 3,300 bits, below what she cannot know. Over a scripted 24-hour operations day at 10 km (96 sessions, with polarization drift, partial and full interception, an 8 dB bend, an altered classical message, and a one-hour fiber cut), the monitor flagged every attack in the first session it touched and the drift after 30 minutes. The bend changed no decision and was visible only as a fall in detections. During the cut every session was rejected, the key store ran dry, and the application was refused until the repair, so the link failed closed. Because only sessions that pass every other check are authenticated, the outage did not drain the authentication key.

## 4.4 Memories and repeaters (Phase 4)

The memory capability matrix (Table 5) is the central result. With an initial fraction of 0.95, a depolarizing memory keeps a pair useful for teleportation for T ln(2.8) ≈ 1.03 T. Atomic ensembles clear only the metropolitan baseline; silicon-vacancy nuclear spins reach geostationary distance; the diamond nitrogen-vacancy carbon-13 register reaches the Moon; and the Mars round trip of 44.6 minutes at maximum range is cleared by three demonstrated memories: the hour-class trapped-ion qubit with less than 1.5× margin at 99 % efficiency, and the two europium-doped crystal memories with wide margin at efficiencies of 1 % and 0.5 %. A pure-dephasing memory acting on a Werner pair drives the fraction to (2f₀+1)/6, which lies below one half, so dephasing alone also ends teleportation, at T₂ ln((4f₀−1)/(2−2f₀)).

A repeater chain of eight segments with one-second memories beats direct transmission beyond about 390 km and stalls when the hold time exceeds the memory; with millisecond memories it never wins. Rate is not the whole answer: the pairs that chain delivers at its crossover have a teleportation fidelity of 0.58, below the classical limit of 2/3, and no nesting depth or purification schedule rescues it. Requiring both a useful pair (F > 2/3) and a rate above direct transmission, and searching up to sixteen segments and two purification rounds per level, the minimum memory coherence time is 4.6 s at 500 km, 39 s at 1000 km, and 23 minutes at 2000 km; no memory helps below about 385 km. Counting retrieval efficiency, which a chain pays at every swap, only the trapped-ion and nitrogen-vacancy carbon-13 memories of Table 5 qualify (389–2242 km and 409–897 km); the europium crystals that clear the Mars round trip fail inside a chain because their retrieval efficiency is 1 % or less. The ranking of memories therefore depends on the architecture: a store-and-forward relay rewards coherence, a nested chain rewards retrieval. Sampling the protocol exactly confirms the closed-form waiting time is exact without nesting and a few percent conservative with it (learn 03/19–03/20). Purification from F = 0.80 to 0.99 by the BBPSSW recurrence, verified against a full 16-dimensional simulation, costs ten rounds and about 2 900 input pairs per output pair; the DEJMPS protocol, also verified numerically, reaches the same target in four rounds and about 32 pairs. Each round is a classical round trip. ## 4.5 Space segment (Phase 5)

A Kepler mean-element ephemeris gives an Earth–Mars range envelope of 0.3711 to 2.6755 au, matching the tabulated constants to 1 %, and a synodic period of 779.9 days. Solar conjunction blocks a 3° line of sight ten times in twenty years for about twenty days each; a relay at Sun–Earth L4 or L5 keeps a path open through every conjunction. A DSOC-class classical terminal delivers tens of megabits per second beyond 2 au, so the two classical bits per teleported qubit are never the bottleneck; light time is. Composed into one budget (`qll/systems/mars_budget.py`), a source of 10¹² pairs per second across a thousand modes, a 0.5 m transmit waist, a 4 m receiver at Mars, 100 nrad pointing, and trapped-ion memories delivers 3.7 × 10⁷ heralded pairs per day at closest approach and 1.1 × 10⁶ at the farthest, with teleportation fidelities of 0.91 and 0.72 after a one-round-trip wait; diffraction is 87 of the 94 dB. Where that source sits is decided by background light, not loss. A 4 m receiver at 1550 nm resolves Earth's disk from Mars, so a single-mode receiver pointed at a ground transmitter sees the radiance of the ground around it; in daylight that is about 190 photons per second per 100 MHz mode against a signal of 0.04 to 1.4, and more than 99 % of heralds would be sunlight. A ground station can operate only with Mars up and the Sun down, a window that is closed on 333 days of each 780-day synodic period, so it fails the requirement of 10⁵ useful pairs on every day outside conjunction (REQ-CAP-003). Displacing the source to about the lunar distance puts Earth 1 to 6 mrad off the receiver's axis, where a stray-light floor of 10⁻⁹ keeps at least 96 % of heralds clean on every day with a link (REQ-CAP-005); reaching 99 % at maximum range needs 2.6 × 10⁻¹⁰, coronagraph-grade optics, which makes the Mars receiver's stray-light control a driving requirement (risk R-9). A relay at L4 that sends one photon to each planet pays diffraction twice and delivers about 2 × 10⁻⁵ pairs per day with the same hardware, so the relay's role is store-and-forward, not dual downlink. Secret key needs no memory: each end measures its photon on arrival (BBM92), so the Werner error rate is set by the source and the background alone, 3.3 % at closest approach and 5.1 % at the farthest, and key flows on every day with a link, from 4.9 × 10⁵ to 2.2 × 10⁷ bits per day (learn 03/21). An earlier version of the budget charged key for the memory wait and wrongly confined it to the months near closest approach.

## 4.6 Application (Phase 6)

The messenger refuses to send when its QKD key buffer is empty rather than downgrading to a computational key, and cannot deliver before d/c. The buffer needed to sustain one message per minute through a Mars-maximum round trip with no key replenishment is about 1.4 kB; a key rate matched to consumption needs almost none. The honest statement of performance is therefore three numbers, not one: key bits per day, round-trip time, and refusals. Over a synodic period the Earth–Mars key supply swings by a factor of 45 with range and stops for 21 days at conjunction, but key is classical once distilled and can be stored. Sized by the sequent-peak rule of reservoir design, a bank of 16.7 MB at each end carries a steady 10⁶ bits per day (125 kB of one-time pad, or about 3,900 session keys) through every day of the cycle without a refusal; with no bank the same demand is refused on 341 of 780 days (REQ-APP-003, TS-7, learn 03/22). The store is cheap; its protection over a wait of up to a synodic period is the open risk (R-10).

## 4.7 Proposals settled in software

Two of the ten proposals could be answered without a bench. For device-independent certification (E10, Table 9), a device reaching 0.95 of the Tsirelson bound needs about 2 500 rounds for a positive finite key at ε = 10⁻¹⁰; one Mars-maximum round trip at one pair per second accumulates about 2 700, so the light-time delay does not by itself prevent Mars from certifying its key, while a device at 0.92 of the bound would need 3 400 rounds and fail at that rate. The pair rate, not the delay, decides. For the transduction trade (E7, Table 10), a 2020-class microwave-to-optical transducer leaves a teleportation fidelity of 0.50 after the link and an optimistic η_t = n_add = 0.1 device leaves 0.65, both below the classical 2/3, whereas a target device with η_t = 0.5 and n_add = 0.01 would preserve 0.95 and out-rate a bare nitrogen-vacancy emitter. The transduction-free architecture is therefore the baseline, with a stated condition for revisiting it: added noise well below efficiency.

## 4.8 Pipelines waiting for data

The analysis for the two requirements that need hardware is implemented and tested on synthetic data. The ODMR fit recovers the zero-field splitting to 0.5 MHz and the field to 0.5 G; the pulsed-control fits recover Rabi, Ramsey, echo, and T₁ parameters; and the T₁(T) fit distinguishes the Orbach–Raman phonon law from the bath-occupation law by orders of magnitude between 77 K and 350 K. The first real spectrum closes Section 5.3; six temperatures close REQ-THM-003.
